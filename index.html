Our Roots

Comprehensive History & Facts

Founding & Early History

Founded in February 1980 by Mark R. Hughes in Los Angeles, California, Herbalife began as a direct-selling company focused on weight management. Following his mother's tragic death related to an eating disorder, Hughes was inspired to build a global multi-level marketing (MLM) model. He famously launched the company by selling the first Formula 1 Protein Shakes from the trunk of his car with the iconic slogan, "Lose Weight Now, Ask Me How."

In 1994, the Herbalife Family Foundation (HNF) was established to support children's nutrition worldwide.

Business Model & Global Reach

Herbalife operates through an MLM model where a network of around 4.5 million independent distributors earn through product sales and team commissions. Since the early 2000s, community-based "Nutrition Clubs" became central to their retail strategy, with over 65,000 clubs operating globally.

The business is registered in the Cayman Islands with corporate headquarters in Los Angeles, California. Today, Herbalife employs approximately 9,900 people and sells its nutrition, weight management, and personal care products in 95 countries.

Leadership Evolution

Following Hughes' death in the 2000s, the company continued its global expansion. On April 25, 2018, it changed its name from 'Herbalife Ltd.' to 'Herbalife Nutrition Ltd.' alongside a two-for-one stock split.

In January 2019, CEO Richard Goudis was dismissed due to pre-CEO comments that contradicted company standards, leading Michael O. Johnson to assume the interim role. John Agwunobi became CEO in March 2020 but stepped down in October 2022, bringing Johnson back as Chairman and Interim CEO.

In May 2025, Stephan Gratziani was appointed CEO, becoming the first former distributor since founder Mark Hughes to lead the company. Michael O. Johnson then transitioned to Executive Chairman.

Regulatory Scrutiny & Controversies

Throughout its growth, Herbalife has faced allegations of operating as a fraudulent pyramid scheme—claims the company has consistently denied. Critics argued distributors were misled about earning potential and were compensated more for recruiting than retail sales. Some studies also raised product safety concerns, particularly regarding potential liver damage.

In the 2010s, facing intense regulatory scrutiny, the FTC launched an investigation in 2014. This culminated in a 2016 settlement where Herbalife agreed to pay a $200 million fine and "fundamentally restructure" its compensation in the US, mandating that distributor rewards be strictly linked to verifiable retail sales.
